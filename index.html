The Donut Paradox: Why Belonging in Robotics Is Still Not the Default (ERF 2027 Workshop)
In-person workshop at ERF 2027 in Birmingham, UK
Home Speakers Schedule Organisers Highlights

Imagine you walk into a robotics lab. All eyes on you:

  1. Because today you brought donuts. Nobody's thinking about your background. You belong there as you are.
  2. Because of your gender, age, country of origin, religion, or neurodivergence. Donuts or not. Because "they" think you don't belong here. And you feel it too.

Despite ample evidence that excellence in robotics knows no gender, origin, or cognitive profile, Scenario (b) remains daily life for many people in robotics: in academia, industry, and entrepreneurship.

This workshop moves past stating the gap. We know the gap exists. Instead, we ask specific, practical questions: what would make Scenario (a) the default? What concrete changes do recruitment, retention, visibility, mentoring, and sponsorship need? How do we build robotics communities where diversity is structural, not accidental? Because diversity in robotics is not only "good to have." Homogeneous research teams have blind spots, making robotics suitable for only part of society, and missing the full potential of the field.

Expected Outcome: A practical diversity, equity, inclusion, and belonging (DEIB) assessment tool that enables robotics teams to map their current state, identify critical gaps, and prioritize actionable next steps.
